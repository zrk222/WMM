# Positioning: one wedge

## The wedge

**Turn one app-growth question into an evidence-backed marketing experiment.**

WMM is the local, owner-approved workspace for that job. It is not sold as a full marketing SaaS, an autonomous growth guarantee or a complete KPI platform.

## First-day and first-month expectations

- **Day 1 — a clearer next marketing move.** Choose one app-growth question and prepare your first evidence packet: a plan, its sources and what is still missing.
- **Around Day 30, with regular use — a record you can build on.** Bring research, approved work and available results into a dated record, then decide what to continue, revise or stop.
- **Potential benefit:** less repeated research, clearer campaign decisions and fewer unsupported bets. Available measurements depend on completed work and connected sources. Growth and time savings are not guaranteed or established by this message.

Use these expectations in the public overview, first-run guide and app introduction. Day 30 is a review horizon, not a promised performance deadline. Exact actions still require their existing approvals.

## Who it is for

Founders and marketing teams promoting and managing one app or a portfolio. Begin with one growth question and one bounded experiment per app. Keep each app’s permissions and evidence separate. This describes the audience and multi-app workflow, not a claim of shared cloud team dashboards.

## Three proof pillars

1. **AI director, human editor.** The agent proposes the plan, drafts and next actions. The owner edits, approves exact content/destinations and keeps every receipt. WMM is not presented as replacing the marketer.
2. **Attribution honesty.** Results are source-bound counts with dates, definitions, denominators and missing coverage. WMM refuses invented D7/D30 numbers, engagement-as-install claims and causal claims without a defensible comparison.
3. **Owner-approved execution.** Permissions are time-limited, app-scoped and revocable. Publishing and spending need separate exact approvals. Default ad spend is zero.

## Message hierarchy

1. **One outcome:** a reviewable experiment tied to one app-growth question.
2. **One workflow:** question → plan → edit/approve → publish → observe → report.
3. **Evidence boundary:** what happened, what is missing and what WMM refuses to claim.
4. **Supporting capabilities:** research, creative, publishing, analytics and agents. Never lead with the full catalog.

## Approved language

- “One app-growth question, one evidence-backed experiment.”
- “Your agent directs; you edit and approve.”
- “Source-bound counts, not invented lift.”
- “Local, owner-approved workspace.”
- “A planning draft is not publication or spend approval.”

## Forbidden language until proven

- “Guaranteed growth”, “best”, “#1”, “fully automated growth”.
- “Fully audited”, “SOC 2 compliant”, “enterprise-grade” or “InfoSec blocker removed”.
- “D7 retention” from cumulative install views or engagement counts.
- “Causal lift” without a predeclared comparison/holdout and mature result.
- “Live provider results” before the actual account receipts exist.

## Objection handling

- **“Is this another AI that posts for me?”** No. It prepares work; you approve exact content and destination.
- **“Can it prove ROI?”** It can retain source-bound counts and a bounded experiment. It will not invent a return number.
- **“Is it enterprise-ready?”** Not yet certified. The security packet is pending independent review; the control map states the gaps.
- **“Why not a full marketing suite?”** The wedge is deliberate. Expansion waits for a real measured result and first-user validation.
